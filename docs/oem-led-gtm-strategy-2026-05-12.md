# OEM-led go-to-market strategy

**Date:** 2026-05-12
**Author:** Wolfpack CTO
**Status:** Supersedes the cold-outbound GTM assumptions in earlier docs. Companion to `wolfpack-team-capabilities-2026-05-12.md` and `dealer-excellence-program-2026-05-12.md`.

This doc replaces the implicit GTM model in earlier strategy docs (founder-meeting deck, F&I Audit spec, Literacy OS strategy doc) where the assumed sales motion was direct-to-dealer cold outbound supported by a sales-lead hire. That model was wrong. The right model is OEM-led, leveraging existing team relationships, with direct-to-dealer as a secondary motion that we accept but do not lead with.

---

## The motion in one sentence

**Wolfpack productizes and scales the work the team already delivers to OEM dealer-excellence programs, with OEM endorsement as the primary distribution channel and direct-to-dealer engagements as secondary opportunity-driven business.**

---

## Why OEM-led

Three structural reasons:

1. **Wolfpack already has the relationships.** PCNA via Hoxsie and senior team. Audi and Kia via Jorge. Toyota and other OEMs through other team-member connections. No comparable software vendor can credibly claim multi-year operating relationships at the OEM level. This is the unfair advantage.

2. **OEMs have mandate authority over dealer networks.** A Porsche or Audi program rolled out at OEM scale is automatically deployed to dealers who are required to comply. Direct-to-dealer cold outbound has to fight for every signed contract. OEM-led has structural distribution built in.

3. **OEM budgets are funded.** Dealer-excellence and training budgets at large OEMs are existing line items. We are not creating demand; we are professionalizing how the demand is served. JD Power and other industry incentives mean OEMs have ongoing reason to fund work that moves these scores.

---

## Lighthouse and pipeline

### Primary lighthouse candidate: PCNA

Wolfpack has won PCNA's JD Power awards multiple years running. The relationship is operating, the trust is established, the work is documented. PCNA is the most likely lighthouse customer for the productized Dealer Excellence Program.

But this is not exclusive. Nothing is off the table. Other entry points include:

- **Audi** via Jorge's relationships.
- **Kia** via Jorge.
- **Toyota** via Hoxsie or other team-member connections.
- **Porsche international** as a sister-region expansion off PCNA traction (if PCNA signs first).
- **Other OEMs** in markets where the team has connections.

### Pipeline diversification principle

Over-indexing on PCNA early creates concentration risk. The pipeline should be developed in parallel: Hoxsie owns PCNA, Jorge owns Audi/Kia, other team members own their specific OEM relationships. The platform we have been building is OEM-agnostic so the product can be co-branded or white-labeled per partner.

## The pitch to the OEM

Generic structure, adapted per OEM:

1. **Open with the existing work.** "We've been delivering [training / dealer-excellence work] to your network for [X years]. Here are the outcomes [JD Power score movement, CSI improvement, named dealer wins]."
2. **Frame the productization.** "We want to professionalize this work into a maintained program with software backbone, so it scales to your full dealer network with consistent quality and measurable outcomes."
3. **Show the F&I Audit as a tangible artifact.** Megan-designed deliverable demonstrates the quality of work-product the OEM would receive across the engagement.
4. **Demonstrate the platform.** Wolfpack Auto + Literacy OS surfaces. Real data flows. Real role-specific translations. Not slideware.
5. **Propose a pilot.** 3-5 dealer rooftops in their network for a 6-month pilot. Measurable success criteria agreed upfront. Pilot extends to full network at year-end if outcomes hit.
6. **Pricing conversation.** Per `docs/dealer-excellence-program-2026-05-12.md`: $1.5M-$5M annual range for full network, multi-year. Outcomes-linked bonus optional.

### What we are NOT pitching

- We are not pitching to compete with CDK or Cox Automotive infrastructure. We are layering on top.
- We are not pitching feature parity with Tekion. We are pitching outcomes that Tekion does not deliver.
- We are not pitching disruption of the dealer's existing DMS. We integrate with what they have.

## CDK / Cox Automotive partnership posture (Decision D3 from prior doc)

The decision is conditional: **partner if it benefits us, do not partner if it harms us**. Specific test cases:

### When partnership benefits us

- CDK or Cox endorses Wolfpack as a recommended training and customer-experience partner for their dealer-network customers. Partnership = top-of-funnel distribution.
- Data integration via official API (rather than scraping) is materially better than the BYO-credentials pattern for that source. Partnership = product quality.
- A specific OEM engagement requires CDK or Cox sponsorship to land. Partnership = sales unlock.

### When partnership harms us

- CDK or Cox requires exclusivity or non-compete clauses that limit our ability to work with other DMS providers or with OEMs directly.
- Partnership comes with revenue-share terms that erode the OEM-engagement margin to unworkable levels.
- Public association weakens our positioning with an OEM that views CDK or Cox as an antagonist.

Default posture: open to partnerships, neutral until the specific terms are reviewed, optimize per opportunity. Do NOT chase partnership as a strategy goal; entertain it as an inbound opportunity.

## Direct-to-dealer as secondary motion

Some direct-dealer opportunities will surface from inbound channels (F&I Audit landing page, trade shows, referrals from existing relationships). These are accepted and served at the smaller-engagement pricing tier, but they are not the GTM motion we build the company around.

Specifically:
- F&I Audit free-deliverable continues at `audits.wolfpackauto.com/fi-penetration` as inbound capture.
- Inbound dealer demos route to Hoxsie or another senior team member.
- Direct-dealer engagements use the same platform spine and the same program structure, sized down to single-rooftop scope.
- We never turn down a real opportunity. We do not invest in cold-outbound infrastructure for direct-to-dealer.

## Cash flow and bridge revenue

OEM sales cycles are notoriously long. 12-18 months from intro to signed PO at large OEMs is realistic. Cash flow has to bridge that gap. Options:

1. **Existing PCNA work** (or equivalent ongoing client engagements) continues as services revenue. This is the most natural bridge.
2. **Inbound direct-dealer engagements** captured through the F&I Audit landing page. Lower ACV but faster close.
3. **Trade-show and conference presence** to surface mid-size dealer-group opportunities (5-20 rooftop groups) that have larger budgets than single rooftops but shorter sales cycles than OEM networks.
4. **CTO and CEO outside consulting** if cash flow stress materializes. Not preferred but available.

Year-one cash-flow plan should be drafted before founder-meeting D1 commit.

## Risks (honest list)

1. **OEM sales cycles are long.** 12-18 months realistic. Year-one revenue from OEM contracts is unlikely. Plan accordingly.
2. **OEM contracts have IP and exclusivity clauses.** Review every contract template before signing. Some clauses can sink future product options.
3. **Single-OEM lighthouse risk.** Diversify pipeline from day one even when growth is slow.
4. **OEM political risk.** Internal champions move on, get reorganized, lose budget authority. Year-one work has to survive a single sponsor change.
5. **Productization of services work has IP overlap with prior client engagements.** Legal review for what is genuinely re-usable vs. client-owned.
6. **Capacity constraint.** 5-7 people running a multi-OEM program is tight. Hire plan needs to be honest about what we can take on in year one without breaking delivery quality.

## Stress tests required before founder green-light

Three customer-discovery validations, all due within 30 days of D1 commit:

1. **One existing OEM contact (PCNA or other), when shown the productized Dealer Excellence Program structure, says: "yes, we would consider this for our dealer network at $X scope."** No vague enthusiasm. Stated scope and indicative budget range.
2. **One existing dealer-principal contact** (from PCNA-affiliated dealers or other team-member relationships), when shown the role-specific translations and program structure, says: "I would advocate for this with my OEM" or "I would pay for this directly at $X/month."
3. **Platform readiness review** — Homyk + Zocchi verify that the Literacy OS engine, F&I Audit, and Wolfpack Auto core surfaces are demo-ready for an OEM pilot. No fabricated demos; only real data flows.

All three should be achievable within 30 days. If any fails, the year-one plan reconsiders before further commit.

## Bottom line

The GTM motion runs through OEM relationships the team already has, not through cold-outbound to dealers. The platform we have been building is the spine of an integrated program offering, not a SaaS product looking for buyers. We do not compete with CDK or Tekion; we layer on top, optionally partner if terms work, and serve a different budget line entirely. The biggest near-term risks are OEM sales-cycle length, team capacity, and IP overlap with prior client work. None are fatal; all need planning.
