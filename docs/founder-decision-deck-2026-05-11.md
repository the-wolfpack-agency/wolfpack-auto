## Slide 3: The Framing Shift

Wolfpack Agency is functionally a portfolio investor.

Consulting revenue funds product development. Products generate IP and recurring revenue. Stop thinking "agency that ships products." Start thinking "product company with consulting cash flow."

Every minute spent on a product that will not return capital is a minute stolen from products that will. Every product we keep alive without revenue is a tax on the ones that could be winning.

## Slide 5: Recommendation in One Breath

Bet on three. Park or hold the rest.

- **Auto** via Wedge E (compliance engine) and Wedge D (overlay on legacy DMS).
- **Instinct** as an AI cost-efficiency platform.
- **LMS** bundled into consulting engagements (no standalone sales motion).
- **Weekend:** activate as client engagement (deal near approval). Treat as productized services, not standalone SaaS.
- **Client sites:** keep. Active service line in production use. Productize the CI/CD spine separately as a reusable internal asset.
- **AgenticQA:** internal-only in 2026, spin out in 2027.

## Slide 6: Why Auto via Wedge E First, Not Wedge A

The original framing was "build a better DMS" (Wedge A). That fight concedes the field to incumbents with 30 years of dealer relationships and OEM certifications we cannot buy on accelerated timeline.

The reframe:
- **Wedge E (compliance engine)** ships fastest. Lowest CAC. Regulatory tailwind (FTC dealer rule enforcement ramping in 2026). One avoided $40K fine pays for two years of subscription. First revenue in 45-60 days.
- **Wedge D (overlay on CDK)** breaks the migration moat that has protected incumbents for 20 years. Dealers do not have to migrate. We sit on top.
- **Wedge A** becomes the destination, not the entry. Once overlay customers depend on us, migration is one click.

The incumbents cannot build any of this without cannibalizing themselves.

---

## Slide 7: Why Instinct is NOT Racing the Labs

Instinct is positioned as an **AI cost-efficiency platform.** Not a Claude Operator competitor. Not a capability play.

The category is unclaimed because **Anthropic and OpenAI structurally cannot compete in it.** Their revenue model depends on customers spending more on tokens, not less. The labs have a fundamental conflict of interest with the "save money on AI" pitch.

The pitch is simple: cut your AI spend 50-80% without losing the outcomes. Pricing tied to savings, not parity. ($499-1,999 / month base plus 30-50% of estimated savings, capped.)

There is a value floor independent of external adoption: **Wolfpack uses Instinct internally for its own consulting operations.** Internal use compounds our consulting margins and gives us a structural edge over agency competitors who pay full freight on AI APIs. Even if external market adoption is slow, the dogfooding pays.

---

## Slide 8: Why LMS Bundles Into Consulting

LMS is a useful product in a crowded category (Cornerstone, Docebo, 360Learning). Low pricing power standalone.

But it is a huge amplifier when bundled. Position it as the persistence layer for change-management work: clients pay for change, LMS captures and reinforces it. Bundle into every engagement. No separate SKU, no separate sales motion.

Target: $500K-2M attached revenue in year one, built into consulting MSAs.

## Slide 9: The Political Tensions (the hard slide)

Three tensions the CEO must weigh in on personally.

**Tension 1: CDK relationships.** Wedge D is adversarial to CDK over time. Wedge A is friendlier (we are a competitor, not a parasite) but strategically weaker. Are CDK relationships an intelligence asset to leverage, or an asset to protect? CEO must choose.

**Tension 2: Instinct positioning.** Must be cost-efficiency from day one, in every conversation. Reject "Operator competitor" framing in all sales and marketing. The labs eat us if we play their game. They cannot touch us if we play ours.

**Tension 3: Consulting versus product.** As products grow, attention and capital shift away from consulting services. Senior agency staff may resist. Frame: products extend the agency's reach and make consulting engagements more valuable. Manage carefully.

## Slide 12: The IP Moat

Honest framing: patents are not the moat here. The techniques underneath all three products have meaningful prior art. We may file cheap provisionals ($1-3K each) as priority-date insurance on genuinely novel combinations, but they are not what protects us.

What actually protects us:

1. **Trade secrets.** Routing thresholds, prompt libraries, model-tier decision logic, customer-success playbooks. Protected by NDAs, access controls, and not publishing full implementations.
2. **Time-to-market on category positioning.** "AI cost-efficiency platform" and "overlay on legacy DMS without migration" are unclaimed positions today. Six to twelve months of head start defines the buyer's mental model.
3. **Customer switching cost.** Once a customer is integrated, leaving means re-instrumenting workflows. Pure engineering moat.
4. **Data network effects.** Customer workloads improve our routing and rule precision. New entrants start cold.
5. **Internal dogfooding credibility.** Hard savings numbers from Wolfpack running on our own infrastructure are proof competitors cannot copy.
6. **Distribution.** CEO relationships, consulting client base, FordDirect via OneMagnify. Distribution beats most software moats over time.

Brand: trademark "Wolfpack Method" and every product name ($250-1000 per mark). Real defensibility for cheap.

## Slide 14: Capital Plan

Four options. The right answer depends on month-6 traction.

- **Option A (bootstrap):** through month 6. Default.
- **Option B (seed):** $2-5M at $15-30M valuation. Run conversations starting month 7 if traction supports.
- **Option C (strategic):** $5-15M at $40-80M from a dealer group, OEM venture arm, or compliance vendor. Keep in peripheral vision.

## Slide 15: Risks (the honest slide)

Top five risks, severity-ranked.

1. **CEO/CTO misalignment on portfolio priorities.** Catastrophic. Mitigated by this meeting and a written decision artifact.
2. **Instinct mispositioned as Operator competitor.** Kills the bet. Lock the cost-efficiency frame in every channel.
3. **CDK legal action on overlay.** Medium likelihood, high severity. Pre-launch legal review, customer-authorized agent framing.

## Slide 18: The Decision Matrix

If we align on the portfolio in the next 14 days, we hit Year-1 targets ($5-15M ARR run rate).

Every month of delay costs us roughly **$500K to $1.5M of Year-1 ARR** (one Phase-1 cohort lost to slipped sales hiring, one Wedge E quarter lost to undecided wedge, one Instinct cohort lost to delayed positioning).

The window for the overlay strategy is 12-18 months. After that, the labs and the incumbents catch up. Entry becomes 10x harder.

Aligning now is cheap. Aligning in 90 days is not.
